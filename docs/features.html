I.

Member records

The foundation. Everything else in the church system will point back at these records.

Full profiling records
Every field from the paper form — personal details, contact, family, committee choices, certification date.
Confirmed
Households and families
Spouse, children, date of marriage. One family, one page.
Confirmed
Children as real records
A child currently appears on two different papers. Here they are one record — and when they grow up, they become a member without being typed in again.
Confirmed
Guardians
Including a guardian who isn't in the household — a grandmother bringing a child whose parents don't attend.
Confirmed
Member numbers
Next free MEM-0001 suggested automatically, and always overridable when a number is already written on the paper.
Confirmed
Scanned forms attached
The photo of the original paper stays with the record, as evidence and for checking against.
Confirmed
Duplicate finder
Warns when a new record looks like one already there. A warning, never a block — two people really can share a name.
Confirmed
Incomplete forms still save
Blanks on paper stay blank. A system that refuses half-filled forms is a system nobody uses.
Confirmed
Nobody is ever deleted
People become inactive, transferred, or deceased. The record stays — it's the church's register.
Confirmed

VII.

Privacy and safety

The form collects birthdates, addresses, civil status, and children's details. Under the Data Privacy Act these are sensitive, and minors' data doubly so.

Five levels of access
ICT, Secretariat, Treasurer, Board, and Committee Chairpersons each see only what their work requires.
Confirmed
Chairpersons see very little
Name, mobile, email — for their own committee only. Not addresses, birthdates, civil status, or families.
Confirmed
Consent recorded per person
Who signed, when, and which version of the form — so the church can actually prove it.
Confirmed
A record of who looked
Every viewing of a person's record is logged. If a member ever asks how their address got out, there's an answer.
Confirmed
Contact details expire
Two years after someone transfers or passes away, their phone, email and address are cleared. Their place in the register stays forever.
Confirmed
Nightly backups
Plus a restore procedure someone has actually tested. A backup nobody has restored from is a rumour.
Confirmed
Two people needed to make a member
The Secretariat can't create logins; ICT can't grant membership. Neither one can do it alone.
Confirmed

IX.

Deliberately left out

Not oversights. Each of these is a decision, and each can be revisited later.

Member logins
The system is built so they can be switched on later without rebuilding anything. Requiring them now would stall the whole project on sign-ups.
Deferred
Member photographs
Photographs of children need their own consent conversation with the Board first.
Deferred
Attendance tracking
There's no attendance form yet, and attendance data is sensitive enough to deserve its own careful design.
Deferred
Text message reminders
Costs money per message, and many forms have no mobile number recorded.
Deferred
Reading handwriting automatically
Checking what a machine guessed takes longer than typing it, and a wrongly read birthdate is worse than a blank one.
Deferred
Merging duplicate records
The system will find duplicates now. Merging waits until there are enough to show what merging should actually do.
Deferred
Fund requests, finances, and minutes
Your EARF, FR, RB, CDV and meeting-minutes forms are the next three phases. They all depend on the member records being in place first.
Deferred
Before Sunday, 23 August

The paper form has been revised

Consent is signed next Sunday, and whatever is printed that day governs the next batch of members. Version 2 of the profiling form is ready — three changes, all made.

  1. Section V, Data Privacy Consent. What the church collects and why, who may see it, how long it is kept, the member's right to access and correct it, and the right to complain to the National Privacy Commission. For a minor, the parent or guardian consents.
  2. A nickname field. Greetings should use the name people actually go by — the Board document already records officers as "MJ" and "JM".
  3. A note on the committee list explaining that Grievance and Reconciliation is appointed by the Board rather than chosen.

Certification is now Section VI, and one signature covers both truthfulness and consent. The footer reads v2 (August 2026) — the registry records which version each member signed. Open it in Word and check the page layout before printing.
